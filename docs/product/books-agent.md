# The Books launch agent

What "good" means for a Books plan, and the first place to look when one disappoints. Shared rules (sources, how to use Qloo, lessons) are in [principles.md](principles.md). Behaviour and numbers are in [spec](spec.md) and [PRD](prd.md); the technical map is [books-overview](../backend/books-overview.md). Film, Music, Games and Podcasts are the models for this one: where a rule is the same, [film-agent.md](film-agent.md), [music-agent.md](music-agent.md), [games-agent.md](games-agent.md) and [podcasts-agent.md](podcasts-agent.md) have the reasoning.

## What a launch plan has to settle

A plan is a set of decisions, not a document:

| Decision | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Where | The markets to lead with and in what order: where the book's readers are strongest, and where the spend and the events go |
| When | The publication date or window, and every beat before it |
| Who | Core and secondary readers, what else they read, watch and hear |
| Which comps | The recent books to pitch against. A comp the team named is kept, and tested for how recent it is |
| Which screen angle | The series and films the readers also watch, and the podcasts they hear, as comparisons for the pitch. A title the team named is ranked beside them |
| Where to sell and gather | Bookshops for events and stock, per city, and the book clubs and communities around them |
| Who pays | Brand partners that could realistically say yes to a book launch, ranked for the readers |
| How to reach them | Reviews, long-lead press, podcasts, creators, festivals, schools and libraries for young readers |
| Cost and measures | A budget split and the numbers to track for this kind of launch |
| Risks | A market that grows slowly, a comp that reads as dated, a date that slips |

## Launch kinds, routes, readerships and formats

A book is launched in one of four ways, through a publisher or on the author's own, for adults, young adults or children, in print, ebook and audio. The agent reads each from the brief. When the brief does not say, it plans a debut, through a publisher, for adult readers, and says so in "To sharpen this plan".

| Kind | Leads with | The calendar | What to track |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Debut** | Comp titles, the screen angle and retail, then press and events | Metadata and cover, the sell-in, advance copies, long-lead press, pre-orders, events, the newsletter and book clubs, creators, publication, the first month | Pre-orders by market and age band, first-week sales, ratings and reviews, where new readers came from |
| **New book by an author** | Comp titles and retail, with the readers of the earlier books told first | The debut calendar, with a note to the earlier books' readers twelve weeks out | Pre-orders against the author's last book, how many readers of the earlier books bought this one |
| **Next book in a series** | Comp titles and the series, retail and creators | The earlier readers first, a price promotion on book one, then the usual calendar without long-lead press | Pre-orders against the last book, sales of book one around the launch, read-through |
| **Reissue or new edition** | Comp titles and the screen angle, retail and creators | The new cover and edition, the tie-in timed to a screen release, the sell-in of the edition | Sales of the new edition against the last, pre-orders around a screen release |

The route, readership and formats then shape what a kind carries:

- **A book a publisher sells** gets Retail and pre-orders, and the sell-in beat. **A self-published book** gets the upload and pre-order beat instead, and a budget weighted to advertising and newsletter swaps.
- **A book for young adults or children** gets Schools and libraries and a beat that sends teachers and librarians a copy. A children's book is bought by adults, so the plan writes to parents, teachers and librarians, and never claims what a child likes.
- **An audiobook** adds the Audiobook section and the release beat. Without one, "To sharpen this plan" says to add it.

Every plan also has Markets, Launch beats, Budget split, What to measure and Risks.

## Requirements (what "done" means)

1. **Plan for the team's decisions.** Their markets, kind, route, readership, formats, age band, comparable books, screen comparisons, bookshops and window are constraints. Test them and say what Qloo found, never replace them. An older comp, or a screen title that ranks low, is said plainly, and the team's choice stays beside the stronger one. Every named market, comp, screen title and bookshop gets an answer.
2. **Work with thin input.** A one-line description, such as "a Nigerian literary fiction debut", is enough. Ask for nothing beyond what the book is, and never ask for comparable books. With no market named, plan for the usual markets of a launch like this; a nationality ("for Nigerians") names the market. End with what would sharpen the plan. A title alone gives the default launch, so the agent asks for one line about the book, and asks again if the answer says nothing.
3. **Describe the readers with books like it, so the person need not.** Comparable books the person names lead. Fewer than three, and the agent adds well known books from the genre (the model proposes them; with no model, the pack has a list per genre), each labelled as added by Oraqul. A book Qloo finds by exact name leads the audience question, and the author never does: a person is not a signal. A debut Qloo does not know is planned from the comparable books, and the plan says so. A title the team named for the screen angle is never added as a comp.
4. **Pitch against recent books.** The shelf lists the recent books these readers pick up, which are the comps worth pitching against. A comp the team named is tested for how recent it is; an older one is kept as a reference with a recent book beside it, never dropped.
5. **Give the screen angle as a comparison, never a promise.** The bridge ranks series, films and podcasts for the readers, each only against titles of its own medium. The plan uses them in the pitch and never says an adaptation exists or is coming.
6. **Cover what Qloo cannot measure from knowledge.** When the brief says who the book is for by language, culture or community, Qloo cannot target it and never receives it (only public entity names, place names and age bands go to Qloo). The model covers it as labelled playbook lines: the language of the pitch, the communities, shops and festivals that reach that audience, and how readers there find books. When search is configured, the web step also looks up where readers in the named markets buy books, and a line may state such a fact only with its source. The plan never claims what that audience likes without a finding, and never stereotypes.
7. **Be date-aware.** Beats are dates when the publication date is known, and weeks before it when not. A beat that is already over is never advised. A festival or prize date or a rival release appears only when a web source states it, with the source. If the publication date has passed, the plan says there is nothing left to plan and points to an audit.
8. **Be realistic about scale.** A new book gets in-kind support from a brand, not sponsorship cash. Who will partner and what the readers like are different questions, and Qloo answers only the second.
9. **Be actionable.** Each line is the move, the reason and who does it. Readable in about five minutes, short lines, plain words, no data jargon in the plan. People who read the book are readers.
10. **Never invent the book.** Its plot, characters, quotes and reviews come only from the brief. What the readers like is what to speak to, never what the book contains.
11. **No forecasts.** No sales, pre-order, chart or advance predictions, and no pricing advice.
12. **Be exportable and revisable.** PDF, Word and Markdown built as documents; a what-if reruns the plan, such as adding a screen comparison, a market or a comparable book.

## What grounded means for Books

- **Examples.** A plain model names the biggest books. A grounded plan names the actual recent books, series, films, podcasts, bookshops and brands these readers rank highest.
- **Screen titles are ranked within one call.** Qloo scores titles of one medium against the readers in one call, so a screen title the team named is scored with the rest and shows where it stands. Scores from separate calls, and from different media, are never compared.
- **The team's assumptions are tested.** The age band, each named market and each comparable book's age are checked against Qloo's data on the comparable books.
- **Where a chatbot beats us.** Qloo has no sales, pre-orders, chart positions, advances, festival dates, retailer terms or BookTok data, so the calendar, retail, creators and festival lines come from publishing knowledge, labelled as such. We win on who the readers are, which recent books they pick up, which screen titles and podcasts they follow, and which bookshops they go to.

## How we judge a plan

Run the same brief through Oraqul and plain models, then score 1 to 5 on:

1. Answers the ask (where to lead, which comps to pitch against, what to do first)
2. Uses what was given (markets, kind, route, readership, formats, age band, comps, screen titles, bookshops, without replacing them)
3. Specific and actionable
4. True (real names and facts only; spot-check them)
5. Knows the readers (credible, by kind of media, not generic)
6. Knows the calendar (the right beats for the kind and the route, with sources for dates)
7. Readable

Qloo can lift only 4 and 5. If we score lower on the rest, the cause is the model, prompt or rules, and is fixable. If we do not win 4 and 5, Qloo is not helping. The grounding report (lines that cite Qloo or a page, findings used, lines dropped, rank kept) counts citations, not whether they are right: a person still reads the plan.

## Known limits

- **Checked on a few genres.** The queries were run live on literary fiction and romantasy briefs, and a whole plan on self help and middle grade briefs ([qloo.md](../backend/qloo.md)). Other genres and markets are not probed one by one; where Qloo has little data the plan says so, and it is not claimed to beat a chatbot there. Re-run `pnpm --filter @oraqul/engine qloo:books-probes` after any change to the queries.
- **Qloo holds age and gender for only about half of books.** A comp with none is left out of the audience read, and the plan counts how many returned a row. A genre tag can stand in, labelled as the genre's.
- **The shelf rests on one list.** Qloo ranks recent books for the comps' readers, tightly: scores sit close together, so the order is a lead and not a margin. A book of the author's own series never fills it, and one book stands for each series.
- **Screen titles are chosen, not found.** Qloo's open lists of series and films for a book audience drift (documentaries and independent dramas), so the bridge scores titles the team named and ones the model or the pack chose. For a memoir, a self help book or other non fiction, no default screen title exists, and the bridge shows the podcasts until a title is named.
- **Podcasts are the books podcasts.** They come from the books tag and are on topic, but a name can be long or oddly listed.
- **Creators and authors are launch practice.** Qloo returned authors for a literary fiction audience and voice actors and narrators for a fantasy one, so the list does not fit every book. Until a list with context fits, the Creators section is playbook and "To sharpen this plan" does not claim it was measured.
- **Bookshops are listed by city.** Qloo returned shops for London, Lagos and New York; a city can return none, and the plan says so.
- **Some books must be named in full.** Qloo lists a book in a series with its series ("Fourth Wing (The Empyrean, #1)"). A name matches with or without that mark. A title with a long subtitle may stand in as the nearest book, and the plan says so.
- **A comp's age is a convention.** Five years holds, ten is weak, older breaks. These are starting values, and an older comp stays in the plan.
- **Markets are cities.** A region such as West Africa is ranked through the cities in Qloo's table that stand for it. A region the table does not cover is not ranked, and the plan says so.
- **Overlap is not a share of readers.** It is how close another audience is to the book's, scored by Qloo, on a scale of 0 to 100.
- **Ambiguous names** are settled in code and the plan says which was chosen. Book names collide often. A wrong match is possible.
